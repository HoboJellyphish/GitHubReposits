Privacy Policy

Family Health Tracker · Last updated: August 2026

The short version: Family Health Tracker does not collect, transmit, or share any data. Everything you enter — profiles, health readings, medications, uploaded documents — is stored only on your own device. There is no account, no server, and no analytics of any kind. The one exception is the optional voice-dictation feature, described below, which on some devices uses your operating system's speech recognition rather than staying fully on-device.

What data the app stores

The app stores the following types of information, entirely on your device, in order to function:

  • Profile details you create (name, avatar, date of birth) for each household member
  • Health entries you log or import: heart rate, sleep, meals, medications and doses, weight, blood pressure, glucose, mood, symptoms, and water intake
  • Documents you choose to upload (e.g. lab results, imaging reports, visit summaries), including any photo or PDF file content
  • Your display and layout preferences for each profile
  • Wearable "connection" records — see the note below on wearable data

Where this data lives

All of the above is saved using your device's local browser storage (or, in the installed Android app, the equivalent on-device app storage). It is not uploaded to any server we operate — because we don't operate one. The app has no backend. Android's automatic cloud backup is explicitly disabled for this app, so your data is not copied to Google Drive either. If you uninstall the app or clear its storage, this data is permanently deleted and cannot be recovered by us, because we never had a copy of it.

Wearable / health-platform data

The app includes a wearable sync feature intended for platforms like Apple Health, Health Connect, Fitbit, Garmin, Whoop, and Oura. At present this feature uses a simulated data source that generates sample data for demonstration purposes — it does not connect to any real third-party account or service, and no data is exchanged with any wearable company. If a future version adds a real connection to one of these platforms, this policy will be updated first to describe exactly what is accessed and how — but any data pulled from a real device will still be stored using the same on-device-only model described above, not sent to us.

Voice input

Every text and number field in the log-entry form has an optional microphone button you can use instead of typing. This is the one feature in the app that is not guaranteed to stay entirely on your device: it uses your browser or operating system's built-in speech recognition, and on many platforms (for example, Chrome on Android) that recognition is performed by a cloud service rather than on the device itself. The app has no control over that — it simply asks the browser to transcribe speech to text, the same way any other page's dictation button would.

Voice input is entirely optional and off unless you tap the microphone icon yourself; the app never listens in the background. Whatever is transcribed appears in the field for you to review, edit, or delete before it is saved — nothing is saved automatically from a voice result. If you never use this feature, it makes no difference to how the app behaves: everything else, including the "works the same in airplane mode" guarantee above, still applies. On Android, the app only requests the microphone permission the first time you tap a voice button, not on install or app launch.

Analytics, advertising, and tracking

None. The app contains no analytics SDKs, no crash-reporting services, no advertising SDKs, and makes no network requests to any third party. There is nothing to opt out of, because nothing is ever sent.

Children's data

Because this app is designed for household/family use, a profile may hold health information about a child. That data is handled exactly like any other profile's data: stored only on the device the app is installed on, under the control of whoever manages that device, and never transmitted anywhere by us.

Permissions

The Android app declares the INTERNET permission, required by the underlying WebView component the app is built on. The app itself makes no outbound network calls of its own — you can verify this by using the app in airplane mode, with one exception: if you tap a voice-dictation mic button, the device's speech recognition may briefly need a connection to produce a transcript (see "Voice input" above). Every other feature, including all logging, charts, and document storage, works identically offline.

The app also declares the RECORD_AUDIO permission, used solely to let the voice-dictation feature access the microphone. Android asks you to approve this the first time you tap a mic button, not at install time, and you can decline it — declining just means voice input won't work; typing still does.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes — for example, when real wearable integrations are added — the update will be posted at this same URL with a revised date above.
